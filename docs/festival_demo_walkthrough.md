# Festival Idea Putrajaya · five-minute fictional KargoKawan walkthrough

This is a **FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION — NOT REAL CARRIER PRICES, QUOTES OR BOOKINGS**. It needs no CSV, courier login or API key. Use only synthetic data. The five-order flow lives in the browser's Streamlit session and never writes to authenticated PostgreSQL pilot jobs, quote drafts or approvals.

1. **0:00–0:50 · Intake and validation.** Open the Streamlit app, find **Festival challenge · five-order fictional walkthrough** near the top of the public fictional area. The **KargoKawan operations assistant** gives one next action: click **Load five-order fictional scenario**. Show five orders from one Kuala Lumpur pickup: three distinct Klang Valley postcodes plus separate George Town and Johor Bahru parcels. Each has a fictional recipient, weight, dimensions, deadline and five-digit postcodes. The green intake message confirms the established CSV validator accepted them. No real customer details are used.
2. **0:50–1:50 · Alternatives and decision.** Read the assistant's **Plan ready** recommendation and next action. Show the alternatives table. It contains five complete fictional standalone parcel assumptions, one explicitly routed three-stop road-vehicle assumption for the three Klang Valley orders, and two tempting cheaper parcel assumptions that miss their deadlines. All quote IDs, providers, prices, transit times and route stops are labelled fictional. The deterministic exact-cover optimiser selects the three-stop vehicle plus two intercity parcels: **RM113.00 assumed total** versus a valid **RM132.00 all-parcel baseline**. The fictional RM19.00 difference is computed from the same eligible assumptions; it is not a real saving or provider quotation. Show selected services, order groups, rejection reasons and solver status.
3. **1:50–2:40 · Review and human approval.** Click **Preview five-order fictional plan**. The assistant changes to **Awaiting approval** and asks the operator to review or discard. Point out the proposal-only notice and absence of a manifest. Click **Approve five-order fictional plan** explicitly. The app rechecks the preview against current orders, quotes and provider availability. It never books or pays for a shipment.
4. **2:40–3:30 · Preparation.** The assistant shows **Approved**. Click **Download fictional preparation manifest — NOT A BOOKING**. Its five rows list fictional recipients, postcodes, deadlines, selected method, quote assumption ID and group total. Click **Mark fictional packages prepared** to show **Preparation started**, a session-only status; no dispatch or carrier tracking is claimed. Real addresses, rates and provider services would require separate verification outside this demonstration.
5. **3:30–4:30 · Disruption and replanning.** Click **Simulate fictional van disruption**. The earlier approval and preparation status clear. The assistant shows **Replan required**, explains the earlier plan is invalidated, and points to a fresh preview. The same optimiser selects five standalone parcels for **RM132.00**, and **What changed** explains the RM19.00 increase and replaced quote IDs. Preview and approve the replacement separately; no decision is silently applied. **Restore fictional van** returns to the original alternatives but still requires a fresh preview and approval. Open **Fictional decision history** to distinguish the proposal, human approval and disruption events.
6. **4:30–5:00 · Constraint honesty.** Select **FEST004** under **Fictional order to adjust deadline**, set **Revised fictional deadline (days)** to **1**, then click **Apply fictional deadline change**. No quoted alternative can meet that intercity deadline, so the assistant says **Blocked**, identifies uncovered orders, shows no valid all-parcel baseline, and prevents proposal approval or manifest export. The fictional decision history records the change.

The original four-order **Competition demo · mixed methods**, the public simulated dashboard, imported-quotation workflows, and the authenticated PostgreSQL pilot remain separate. The authenticated pilot continues to reject synthetic quotes. EasyParcel OAuth remains disabled and the workflow performs zero carrier calls. Rates, ETAs, capacity and multi-stop service are internally consistent *assumptions* solely for illustrating software behavior; actual availability and savings are unverified. The session-only demonstration resets when its browser session is lost. The UI uses the existing layout; a visual redesign is outside this phase.

The operations assistant is a deterministic explanation layer. It reads the existing order validator, quote eligibility review, exact-cover solver result, preview and approval flags, provider availability and session event history. It does not invoke an LLM, authenticate fictional prices, predict a guaranteed arrival or perform carrier actions. Its cost comparison appears only when the optimiser finds a valid all-parcel baseline; solver-limited feasible results are identified without a global optimality claim. The next visual phase can improve typography, layout, navigation, responsive behavior and the density of the alternatives table.
